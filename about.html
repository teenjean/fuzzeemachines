As you probably already know, my work surrounds the ever-evolving relationships between craft and computation, fiber and electronics, programmable soft and biomaterials, DIY computing, data and poetry - I could go on. So, what is this space? Yet another outlet to showcase more work? Hmmm - yes and no.


As the days have rolled by, I've been wondering: At world's end, what would we preserve? What would I preserve? Given the current state of the world, preservation of histories, curiosity, artifacts has, I think, become increasingly important. Not to mention the spaces on the web that are still here, but long forgotten e.g. MySpace, Limewire, "ugly" websites. Or are these things meant to "die" an undying death. When Arlo Iron Cloud, a broadcaster for KILI Radio, the voice of the Lakota Nation on the Pine Ridge Reservation, was posed the question what might he preserve to restart the U.S. Nation following a nuclear apocalypse, he responded he might burn the Constitution and the Bill of Rights. He argued true democratic ideals survive dynamically through oral storytelling and community connection.
